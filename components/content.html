Shapes

  • Success, info and neutral: a dot. Warning: a diamond. Danger: a square. Muted: a ring.
  • High contrast adds a border to every chip.

Chip style

  • Outline with an icon (style B, chosen on the UX-04 board).

Where status goes

  • A heading: .title-status, chips straight after the name, centred on its line. Never on a line of their own under it. Details such as a version go on the right.
  • A list of things with a state: .status-icon straight after the name, the word in hidden text and a tooltip.
  • A row with actions: the chip in .row__side. A card: in .card__meta. A stat tile: under the value. A cover: its corner.
  • At most two chips in one place; more belongs in a facts list.

Tooltip

The name of something that shows only an icon, on hover and on keyboard focus. The pointer can move onto it, Escape closes it, and it stays until the pointer or focus leaves (WCAG 1.4.13). Status icons in lists use it.

RadarrConnected LidarrNeeds attention

When to use it

  • The name of an icon button, and the word of a status icon.
  • A short name only: a few words, no full stop.

When not to

  • Anything needed to act or decide: put it in the page, or in help (.help-links).
  • Links, buttons with words, or anything on a phone that has no other way to show it.
  • The title attribute: keyboards cannot reach it.

Accessibility

  • The words are already the thing's name (aria-label or hidden text), so the tooltip is hidden from screen readers and never adds anything new.
  • It shows at once on keyboard focus and after 0.3 seconds on hover; Escape closes it without closing a dialog behind it.
  • A status icon has tabindex="0" so keyboards reach its tooltip.

Classes and behaviour

  • data-tooltip="…" on the thing; one .tooltip for the page, made the first time one shows.
  • The page is listened to only while a tooltip shows.

When to use it

  • A sparkline: the shape of the last hour or day beside a number, in a stat tile.
  • A gauge: one value against a known most, such as a percentage or a safe temperature.

When not to

  • Reading values off it, or comparing series: use the line chart (.chart) with its table.
  • A count with no most: use the number alone. Progress of a job: use the progress bar.

Accessibility

  • The drawing is hidden from screen readers; .stat__trend and .gauge__value carry the numbers in words.
  • A gauge's colour changes at data-warn and data-danger; the tile then has a status chip, so the state is never colour alone.
  • Lines are 2px in the chart colours, at least 3:1 against the tile; Windows Contrast draws them in system colours.

Classes and behaviour

  • svg.sparkline[data-values] (data-unit, data-period, data-min, data-max), .stat__trend; .gauge[data-value], __dial, __svg, __track, __fill, __value, __label.
  • SharedComponents.sparkline(svg, values, options) and gauge(el, value) to update them.

Pagination

Pages of a long table: where you are in words, Previous and Next, the first and last pages and those either side of this one, and how many rows a page shows.

Download history

NameFinishedSize

When to use it

  • Tables people sort, compare or come back to by place: history, logs as a table, search results in a table.
  • More than about 50 rows.

When not to

  • Walls and lists people browse: use load more as you scroll.
  • Fewer rows than one page: leave it out.

Accessibility

  • A nav named for what it pages; the current page has aria-current="page"; Previous and Next say "page" in hidden text; the gaps are hidden from screen readers.
  • Links are 44px targets. Changing page in place says the new place politely and moves focus to the table's heading.
  • Rows per page is a labelled select that keeps the first row in view.

Classes and behaviour

  • .pagination, __summary, __pages, __link, __gap, __size.
  • SharedComponents.pagination(nav, {{ page, pageSize, total, noun, sizes, href, onPage, onSize, focus }}), or the same markup from the server with real links.

Rules

  • Errors say what happened and what to do, in the page, not only in a toast.
  • Empty states say what is missing, why, and the one next step.
  • Skeletons have the shape of the content; never a spinner on its own for a whole page.

Roles

  • Success and info: role="status". Errors that block: role="alert".

In script

  • SharedComponents.notice("warning", "Disk 3 needs attention", "12 sectors were reallocated this week.", { actions: button }) draws a notice with its icon and role. Pass alert: true for an error that blocks.

Toasts and saved notes

A toast is a short message about something that happened: a card in the corner with a coloured edge, a title, an optional line of detail, one optional action and Close. Success, information, warning or error. On settings pages a success is a saved note beside the button instead. An error that belongs to something on the page (a field, a row) also shows there.

Select Save changes: the note appears beside it and goes at the next change.

Toast

  • Bottom right, full width on phones. Up to three stack, newest at the bottom.
  • Success (tick), information (i), warning (triangle) or error (circle, on a red tint): icon, edge and words, never colour alone.
  • Title in the past tense, no full stop: "Follow saved". Detail is one short line.

Timing

  • Success and information: 5 seconds, 10 with an action. Warning: 10 seconds. Error: until it is closed. Each waits while the pointer or keyboard focus is on it.
  • Errors are read out at once (role="alert"), the rest politely (role="status"), without moving focus. A new toast never pushes out an error.

Saved note

  • Settings pages only: "Saved at 16:31", beside the button that saved. Stays until the next change.

Code

  • SharedComponents.toast(title, { detail, tone, action: { label, run } }); tone is success, info, warning or danger
  • SharedComponents.toast.error(title, options), toast.warning(...)
  • SharedComponents.savedNote(element)
  • .toasts, .toast[data-tone], .toast__text, __title, __detail, __action, __close, .saved-note

Callouts

A notice in help and long text. One colour means one thing, and the first words say it, so the meaning never rests on colour. No steps and no emoji inside a callout.

Before you begin:

Sign in as an administrator, and have your Jellyfin API key to hand.

Limitation:

Listening progress copies across only for books that are in both libraries.

Warning:

Deleting a backup removes it from the disk. It cannot be restored.

Tip:

Activate alerts so Starboard tells you when a disk fills up.

Example:

Alex keeps films on disk 1 and music on disk 2, so a full disk stops only one of them.

Meaning

  • Info: before you begin. Warning: a limitation. Danger: cannot be undone. Success: a tip. Muted: an example or aside.

Classes

  • .notice, .notice[data-tone="warning|danger|success|muted"], .notice__title, .notice__body

Log viewer

An app's log as monospace lines: filter by level, search with the matches marked, follow new lines as they come in, wrap long lines and download the whole log. It keeps the newest 10,000 lines and draws only the newest 1,000 that match, so it stays light on a small server. This example holds 10,000 made-up lines.

Jellyfin log

    While following, a new error is said politely.

    When to use it

    • An app's or a job's log on its own page or in a wide slide-out, where people find out what went wrong.
    • Logs that grow while people read them.

    When not to

    • A short history of events: use the timeline or event rows.
    • Search across many apps' logs: send people to the log file or a log tool.

    Accessibility

    • The lines are an ordered list in a scrolling region that the keyboard can reach and scroll; levels are words, with an edge on errors and warnings.
    • New lines are never read out; only new errors are, politely, and only while following. Scrolling up pauses following, and the count line says so.
    • The level buttons are pressed or not, with their counts in their names; the search says how many lines match.

    Classes and behaviour

    • .log-view[data-log-view], __bar, __search, __count, __scroll, __lines, __line[data-level], __time, __level, __text, __match; [data-log-level], [data-log-follow], [data-log-wrap], [data-log-download].
    • SharedComponents.logView(box, {{ lines, maxLines, show, fileName }}) returns add(lines) and clear().

    Drop shadows on and off

    DASH-536. One switch, data-shadows="off" on the page, sets --shadow-card and --shadow-raised to none, so cards, covers, menus, dialogs and slide-outs lose their shadow and keep their border. Left: shadows on. Right: the same parts with the switch on. An app asks in its set-up (a small install starts with the shadows off) and keeps the choice with the person.

    Shadows on (default)

    Card

    Border and layered shadow.

    Shadows off

    Card

    Border only; nothing else changes.

    Rules

    • Only the two shadow tokens change; the 3px accent bars and hover edges are inset lines, not drop shadows, and stay.
    • The note beside the switch: "Drop shadows cost a little processor on every scroll; turning them off helps small and older computers".
    • High contrast already has none.
