Components

One library for Storyarr and Starboard: tokens, components, behaviour and icons. The guides cover content design, accessibility and security checks.

Colour

Role names only. Components never use a raw colour, so a theme is a change of values. Every text pair meets WCAG 2.2 AA (4.5:1) in Light, Dark and High contrast.

Use

Accent

  • Teal (UX-02, option B); changing it is one token in each theme.

Scroll bars

  • Thin and square in every scrolling area, in --color-scrollbar (the border colour; the text colour in High contrast) on a clear track. They stay visible; only shelves hide theirs, behind arrows.

Chart series

  • --series-1 to --series-7 in a fixed order that follows the thing, never its rank. Checked for colour blindness, and 3:1 against the surface, in all four themes.
  • --series-empty with a hatch for free space.

Rules

Use

  • Nothing else goes in the header: page actions live in the page head or the section toolbar.
  • Tabs that do not fit show icons only (.is-compact) with the names as tooltips; the 2nd level scrolls sideways.
  • A count on a tab says what it counts in hidden text.

Search button

  • Search is always one button away (David, 4 November 2026): .rail__search, first in the rail under the logo, moves focus to the header's search box.
  • On phones, where the box is hidden, .site-header__search-btn in the header opens the same box as a row under the header. Escape or a press outside closes it and returns focus.
  • Both buttons have data-search-open and aria-controls naming the search form; components.js does the rest. No single-key shortcut, so nothing fires while typing (WCAG 2.1.4).

Accessibility

  • Each tab row is a nav with its own name; the current tab has aria-current="page" and a 3px bar, not colour alone.
  • Search is a plain box with a hidden submit button, so voice control can say "Search". The phone search button says "Search" and reports aria-expanded.

Display button

The same button and slide-out in every app: the last item in the header, on every page, Sign in and Set up included. It opens Display and accessibility.

The button

  • .icon-btn, the sun icon and the word Display. On phones the word is hidden from sight but still read out.
  • aria-haspopup="dialog" and aria-expanded.

The slide-out

  • Titled Display and accessibility; Close is named "Close display and accessibility".
  • In this order: Theme (Match device, Light, Dark), Text size (100% to 175%), Reduce motion (Match device, On, Off), High contrast, Underline links, Wider text spacing. An app adds its own after these (Starboard: Design).
  • Choices apply at once. Done and Reset in the foot.

Saving

  • To the person's account where the app has accounts; otherwise in the browser.
  • components.js applies the choices from data-display inputs. data-display-save="key" on the form keeps them in the browser; data-display-reset="key" on Reset.

44px high, square, bold 0.875rem. One primary button per view. Words say what happens: "Save connection", not "OK".

Menu button

Rules

  • Errors say what happened and what to do, in the page, not only in a toast.
  • Empty states say what is missing, why, and the one next step.
  • Skeletons have the shape of the content; never a spinner on its own for a whole page.

Roles

  • Success and info: role="status". Errors that block: role="alert".

Toasts and saved notes

A toast is a short message about something that happened: a card in the corner with a coloured edge, a title, an optional line of detail, one optional action and Close. Success, information, warning or error. On settings pages a success is a saved note beside the button instead. An error that belongs to something on the page (a field, a row) also shows there.

Select Save changes: the note appears beside it and goes at the next change.

Toast

  • Bottom right, full width on phones. Up to three stack, newest at the bottom.
  • Success (tick), information (i), warning (triangle) or error (circle, on a red tint): icon, edge and words, never colour alone.
  • Title in the past tense, no full stop: "Follow saved". Detail is one short line.

Timing

  • Success and information: 5 seconds, 10 with an action. Warning: 10 seconds. Error: until it is closed. Each waits while the pointer or keyboard focus is on it.
  • Errors are read out at once (role="alert"), the rest politely (role="status"), without moving focus. A new toast never pushes out an error.

Saved note

  • Settings pages only: "Saved at 16:31", beside the button that saved. Stays until the next change.

Code

  • SharedComponents.toast(title, { detail, tone, action: { label, run } }); tone is success, info, warning or danger
  • SharedComponents.toast.error(title, options), toast.warning(...)
  • SharedComponents.savedNote(element)
  • .toasts, .toast[data-tone], .toast__text, __title, __detail, __action, __close, .saved-note

Callouts

A notice in help and long text. One colour means one thing, and the first words say it, so the meaning never rests on colour. No steps and no emoji inside a callout.

Meaning

From the right, over a dimmed page. For the details of one thing, and for editing it, without losing your place in the list. What is Usenet?

Usenet is a network of servers that keep copies of files people post. You pay a Usenet provider for access, and an indexer lists what is there.

What you need

  • A Usenet provider, which you pay for each month.
  • An indexer, which lists the files.
  • A Usenet app, such as SABnzbd, which fetches them.

What this step does

It installs the Usenet app and connects it to the app, so each download goes into the right folder.

Help is a slide-out: the step stays in view beside it. Only this text scrolls; the heading, Close and Back to set-up stay in view. A press outside closes it.

Shape

  • Progress bar, then the step bar for a step in parts, then the step. The step bar is as wide as the progress bar, with a line between parts; parts are numbered 1, 2, 3 and each is a step in the count (here steps 6 to 8 of 18). On phones only the numbers show, with ticks for done parts.
  • Review aids (What happens next, decision tree) only while testing: beside the step from 1100px, under it below. Without them the step has the whole width.
  • The first question asks which job (new install, restore, migration); each has its own wizard (.wizard-paths).

Steps

  • One question per step; steps that do not apply are left out, and the tree says why.
  • Option cards, one marked "(recommended)" with the reason. Never "easiest".
  • Continue first, then Back, then Skip this step.

Back up your settings

How-to

Save a copy of Starboard's settings, so you can put them back after a new install. It takes about a minute.

Before you begin

  • Sign in as an administrator. Read Choose who can open Starboard.

Steps

  1. Go to Settings, then Backup.
  2. Select Back up now. A message says when the copy is saved.
  3. Select Download the backup (ZIP) to keep a copy on another computer.
Tip:

Activate 'Back up every night' and Starboard keeps the last 7 copies for you.

Things to note

  • A backup holds settings only.
    • Films, music and books stay on their disks and are not copied.
    • To copy those too, read Back up the server.
